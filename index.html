Inside the island

Everything lives right by the camera.

Hover to open it, move away to close it. Click to keep it open. When something happens, it says so and gets out of the way.

  1. See what's playing in Apple Music or Spotify, with artwork and a little equalizer beside the camera. Hover for play, pause, skip and a scrubber.

  2. Drag files onto the notch to hold them while you find where they go. The island holds the actual file, keeps it through restarts, and puts it back in its original folder when you ask.

    AirDrop and dragging into other apps share a copy, so the held file stays put.

  3. When AirPods connect, a banner shows left, right and case battery. Connect or disconnect any paired accessory from the notch.

  4. Switch between speakers, AirPods, a display or AirPlay, and set the volume or mute in the same place.

  5. Banners when you plug in, unplug, run low or finish charging, with time to full or time left whenever macOS knows it.

  6. Pick 5, 15, 25 or 45 minutes. The countdown sits next to the camera, with pause, +5 and stop, and “Time's up” when it ends.

  7. A green dot while a camera is on, orange while a microphone is.

    Microphone detection needs macOS 14.2 or later.

Install

Open it in about a minute.

Rishik builds the app himself and it isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS asks you to confirm it once. Here's exactly what you'll see.

  1. Download the ZIP

    Click Download for Mac. Safari saves it to Downloads and usually unzips it for you. If it doesn't, double-click the ZIP.

  2. Drag it into Applications

    Open Finder, then drag Dynamic Island from Downloads into Applications.

  3. Open it, then allow it in Settings

    Double-click the app. macOS says it can't verify it, so click Done.

    “Dynamic Island” Not Opened Apple could not verify “Dynamic Island” is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy.
    DoneMove to Trash

    What the warning looks like (recreated)

    Now open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to Security, and click Open Anyway. Click Open Anyway again and enter your Mac password.

    “Dynamic Island” was blocked to protect your Mac.Privacy & Security → Security
    Open Anyway

    The button shows up for about an hour after you try to open the app. If you don't see it, open the app again and come back.

  4. Control-click, then Open

    In Applications, hold control and click Dynamic Island. Choose Open, then click Open in the dialog. You only do this once.

    If that doesn't offer Open, use System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway instead.

  5. Allow Bluetooth, and you're done

    The island appears around your notch. Allow Bluetooth when asked so AirPods banners work. Music and Spotify ask for permission the first time you press a play button.

    Nothing else is requested. Sounds are off until you turn them on in the island's … menu.

Still blocked?

Paste this into Terminal. It removes the “downloaded from the internet” flag from this one app and changes nothing else on your Mac.

$ xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Dynamic Island.app"

Check the download

Want to be sure the ZIP is the real one? Run this on it before you open it. Safari may unzip and remove the ZIP; if so, download it again with “Open safe files” turned off.

$ shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/Dynamic-Island.zip

It should print bbc32bc0a50427cccdfcba683820b4c2ff1b4952a7026a519739fb2ed72186ee, the same SHA-256 as on the release page.

Honest by design

It stays on your Mac.

What it does

  • No account, no ads, no analytics. It doesn't phone home.
  • One network request, for album art. Spotify artwork loads from Spotify's own image server. Nothing else goes online.
  • Public macOS features only. No hacks, no private APIs, nothing that breaks with an update.
  • Two permissions, asked when needed. Bluetooth for AirPods, and Music or Spotify control the first time you use it.
  • Quiet by default. Sounds stay off until you switch them on.

What it won't pretend to do

  • Replace the volume and brightness pop-ups. That needs Accessibility access, and the app doesn't ask for it.
  • Show notifications or calls. macOS has no public way to read other apps' notifications.
  • Change AirPods noise control. There's no public way to do it, so the island links to Bluetooth settings.
  • Control YouTube, Podcasts or browsers. It works with Apple Music and Spotify.
  • Name the app using your camera. macOS only says that a camera is on.
Questions

Good questions.

Is it free?

Yes. No trial, no account, no upgrade screen.

Which Macs does it run on?

Macs with Apple silicon (M1 or later) running macOS 13 Ventura or later.

It's made for MacBooks with a notch, and it still works on other displays: the island drops down from the top center when something happens, and the menu bar icon opens it any time.

Why does macOS warn me about it?

Apple only skips that warning for apps it has notarized, and notarizing needs a paid Apple developer account. This build is signed by the person who made it, not yet by Apple, so macOS asks you to confirm once. After that it opens like any other app.

Where do files on the shelf go?

Dropping a file moves it into the app's own folder until you drag it out, use Move To… or Put Back. It stays there through restarts, and it never replaces a file that's already at the destination.

The folder is ~/Library/Application Support/com.rishikrontala.DynamicIsland/FileShelf. It's storage, not a backup, so put files back before deleting the app.

Can I turn parts of it off?

Yes. The island's … menu has switches for opening on hover, music in the notch, device banners, charging banners, camera and mic dots, sounds, haptics and Launch at Login. Colors are in the same menu.

How do I update it?

Download the new version and drag it into Applications, replacing the old one. Files on the shelf stay where they are. macOS may ask for Bluetooth again, because each build is signed separately.

How do I remove it?

Open the shelf and click Put Back All first. Then quit from the menu bar icon and drag the app from Applications to the Trash.
